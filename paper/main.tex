\begin{abstract}
Clinical AI predictions carry varying confidence, yet FHIR R4, the dominant standard for clinical data exchange, provides no mechanism to express assertion-level uncertainty from multiple AI models. Simultaneously, compliance assessment under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) is conventionally treated as binary, despite pervasive uncertainty in Protected Health Information classification, de-identification completeness, minimum necessary scope, and business associate safeguards. We present a HIPAA compliance algebra grounded in J{\o}sang's Subjective Logic that models compliance status as uncertain epistemic states rather than binary labels. The algebra defines four operators, each mapped to specific HIPAA provisions: PHI Classification Confidence, De-identification Assessment (Safe Harbor and Expert Determination), Minimum Necessary Rule, and Business Associate Agreement (BAA) Trust Chain. We prove algebraic properties for each operator and demonstrate dual-regime GDPR and HIPAA composition for multinational health systems. We further present a proof-of-concept for privacy-preserving compliance aggregation using CKKS homomorphic encryption, achieving single-operation accuracy within $1.19 \times 10^{-7}$ of plaintext at 27\,ms latency. All operators are implemented within \texttt{jsonld-ex}, an open-source Python library that also carries opinions inside FHIR R4 resources through a standard extension, and evaluated on synthetic FHIR R4 data generated by Synthea. Property-based testing across 10,000 random opinions confirms all proven properties with zero counterexamples.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%===================================================================

Clinical AI systems produce predictions that inform medical decisions: diagnostic classifications, medication recommendations, risk stratifications, and entity extractions from clinical text. Each prediction carries a confidence that varies across models, patient populations, and clinical contexts, and predictions from multiple models may agree, partially overlap, or conflict.

Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources Release 4 (FHIR R4)~\cite{hl7fhirr4}, the dominant standard for clinical data exchange, offers limited mechanisms for representing this uncertainty. The \texttt{RiskAssessment} resource provides a \texttt{probability} field as a decimal, but this scalar value cannot distinguish between fundamentally different epistemic states: strong conflicting evidence, genuine ignorance, and moderate confidence with well-characterized uncertainty.

The same limitation afflicts compliance assessment under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)~\cite{hipaa_privacy}, which establishes privacy and security requirements for Protected Health Information (PHI). Compliance is conventionally evaluated as a binary classification. In practice, compliance assessment involves compounding uncertainty: whether all 18 Safe Harbor identifier categories have been removed, whether a business associate's safeguards are adequate, and whether the minimum necessary standard has been met.

We propose a HIPAA compliance algebra grounded in J{\o}sang's Subjective Logic (SL)~\cite{josang2016subjective} that addresses both gaps. SL represents beliefs as opinions $\omega = (b, d, u, a)$ where belief $b$, disbelief $d$, and uncertainty $u$ satisfy $b + d + u = 1$, and $a$ is a prior base rate.

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A HIPAA compliance algebra with four operators (PHI Classification, De-identification Assessment, Minimum Necessary Rule, BAA Trust Chain), each grounded in specific HIPAA provisions, with proofs.
\item Dual-regime composition of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)~\cite{gdpr2016} and HIPAA via Jurisdictional Meet, for multinational health systems.
\item A proof-of-concept for privacy-preserving compliance aggregation using CKKS~\cite{cheon2017ckks} homomorphic encryption.
\item Empirical evaluation on synthetic FHIR R4 data~\cite{walonoski2018synthea}, property-based verification across 10,000+ random opinions, and a sensitivity study.
\end{enumerate}

All operators are implemented within \texttt{jsonld-ex}~\cite{syed2026jsonldex}, an open-source Python library extending JSON-LD 1.1~\cite{jsonld11} with SL-grounded confidence.

\textbf{Scope caveat.} We are computer scientists proposing a formal model, not legal scholars; every mapping from a HIPAA provision to an algebraic operation involves interpretive choices that compliance professionals may dispute.

\section{Background and Related Work}
%===================================================================

\subsection{Subjective Logic}

A \emph{binomial opinion} about proposition $X$ is a tuple $\omega_X = (b, d, u, a)$ where $b + d + u = 1$ and $a \in [0, 1]$ is the base rate~\cite{josang2016subjective}. The \emph{projected probability} $P(\omega) = b + a \cdot u$ provides a point estimate that reverts to the base rate under complete uncertainty. Besides the meet defined below, we employ two SL operators: \emph{trust discount} (propagating through trust chains) and \emph{opinion decay} (temporal degradation).

\subsection{FHIR R4 and the Uncertainty Gap}

FHIR R4~\cite{hl7fhirr4} defines over 140 resource types for clinical data exchange. The \texttt{RiskAssessment.probability} field accepts a decimal value, but a probability of 0.5 could mean ``strong evidence for and against'' ($b$ and $d$ both high, $u$ low), ``complete ignorance'' ($b$ and $d$ low, $u$ high), or ``moderate confidence,'' distinctions that decide whether additional tests are ordered, treatment proceeds, or a specialist is consulted.

\subsection{HIPAA Compliance Formalization}

Existing approaches fall into three categories. \emph{Checklist-based tools} translate provisions into binary questionnaires and aggregate pass counts as percentages; they discard epistemic nuance by treating ``no evidence gathered'' and ``evidence of violation'' identically as non-compliant. \emph{Risk-scoring frameworks} such as NIST SP 800-66 assign ordinal risk levels (low, medium, high) that lack algebraic structure and cannot be composed across organizational boundaries or derivation chains. \emph{Formal policy languages} encode access control rules for PHI disclosure but model authorization decisions, not the uncertainty of the compliance assessment itself.

Subjective Logic has been applied to legal evidence weighing in judicial proceedings~\cite{josang2016subjective}, which addresses evidentiary uncertainty in adjudication rather than compliance status uncertainty in regulatory assessment. The \texttt{jsonld-ex} library~\cite{syed2026jsonldex} implements a GDPR compliance algebra on the same SL foundation; our HIPAA algebra extends it to the US healthcare setting with operators for PHI classification, de-identification methods, the minimum necessary standard, and business associate chains.

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Healthcare Computation}

Fully homomorphic encryption, first demonstrated by Gentry~\cite{gentry2009fhe}, enables computation on encrypted data without decryption; the CKKS scheme~\cite{cheon2017ckks} added approximate arithmetic over encrypted floating-point values, making it practical for statistical and machine learning workloads. Healthcare applications include encrypted logistic regression, privacy-preserving genomic analysis, and secure federated learning across hospital networks, all of which compute on \emph{clinical data}. Our contribution applies HE to a different layer, \emph{compliance metadata}: aggregate compliance opinions across organizational units without revealing individual department assessments, complementary to and composable with encrypted clinical computation. We use TenSEAL~\cite{tenseal2021}, a Python wrapper around Microsoft SEAL~\cite{seal2022}.

\section{HIPAA Compliance Algebra}
%===================================================================

\textbf{Definition 1 (Compliance Opinion).} $\omega = (l, v, u, a)$ where $l$ is belief of lawfulness, $v$ is belief of violation, $u$ is uncertainty, and $a$ is the base rate, with $l + v + u = 1$.

Each operator below names the provision of the HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 164)~\cite{hipaa_privacy} that it encodes; the two de-identification methods follow the HHS guidance on de-identification~\cite{hhs2012deid}, and the business associate chain follows the contract requirements of \S164.504(e). The provision-to-operator mapping was reviewed by the hospital-based co-author but not by legal counsel; its interpretive choices are acknowledged as limitation (1) in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

We reuse the Jurisdictional Meet $J_\sqcap$ of the GDPR compliance algebra implemented in \texttt{jsonld-ex}~\cite{syed2026jsonldex}:

\textbf{Definition 2 (Jurisdictional Meet).} Given $\omega_1, \omega_2$:
\begin{align}
l_\sqcap &= l_1 \cdot l_2, \quad
v_\sqcap = v_1 + v_2 - v_1 v_2 \label{eq:jmeet}\\
u_\sqcap &= (1{-}v_1)(1{-}v_2) - l_1 l_2, \quad
a_\sqcap = a_1 a_2 \nonumber
\end{align}

$J_\sqcap$ is a conservative variant of J{\o}sang's binomial multiplication, the SL conjunction operator~\cite{josang2016subjective}: the two share $v_\sqcap$ and $a_\sqcap$, and they coincide in every component when $a_1 = a_2 = 0$. Binomial multiplication additionally transfers base-rate-weighted mass from uncertainty into belief so that $P(\omega_1 \cdot \omega_2) = P(\omega_1) P(\omega_2)$; $J_\sqcap$ keeps that mass in uncertainty, so $P(\omega_\sqcap) \leq P(\omega_1) P(\omega_2)$, the difference being $a_2 l_1 u_2 (1 - a_1) + a_1 u_1 l_2 (1 - a_2) \geq 0$. We use the conservative form because a favorable prior must not be converted into lawfulness belief: belief in a conjunction of compliance requirements is earned only from belief in every requirement. Definition~2 extends to $n$ opinions by iteration; since $l$, $1 - v$, and $a$ multiply, the result is independent of order and grouping, with $l = \prod l_i$, $v = 1 - \prod (1 - v_i)$, and $u = \prod (1 - v_i) - \prod l_i$.

\subsection{Operator H1: PHI Classification Confidence}

HIPAA \S164.514(b)(2) defines 18 identifier categories for Safe Harbor de-identification. Data is PHI-free only if all identifiers are absent.

\textbf{Definition 3.} $\text{PHI}(\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_n) = J_\sqcap(\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_n)$

\textbf{Theorem 1.} (a)~$l + v + u = 1$, all $\geq 0$. (b)~$l_{\text{PHI}} \leq \min(l_i)$. (c)~With uniform $l_i = p$: $l_{\text{PHI}} = p^n$. (d)~If any $v_i = 1$, then $v_{\text{PHI}} = 1$.

\emph{Proof.} (a)~$l + v + u = \prod l_i + 1 - \prod(1 - v_i) + \prod(1 - v_i) - \prod l_i = 1$; $l$ and $v$ are nonnegative by construction, and $u \geq 0$ because $l_i \leq 1 - v_i$ for every $i$. (b), (c)~Immediate from $l = \prod l_i$ with factors in $[0, 1]$. (d)~$v_j = 1$ zeroes $\prod(1 - v_i)$. $\square$

Table~\ref{tab:phi_degradation} shows the exponential degradation: even at $p = 0.99$ per identifier, six present identifiers yield $l = 1.38 \times 10^{-8}$.

\subsection{Operator H2: De-identification Assessment}

\textbf{Safe Harbor} (\S164.514(b)(2)): $\text{DeidentSH} = J_\sqcap(\omega_{r_1}, \ldots, \omega_{r_{18}})$, producing $l_{\text{SH}} = \prod l_{r_i}$.

\textbf{Expert Determination} (\S164.514(b)(1)):
$\text{DeidentED} = \text{trust\_discount}(\omega_{\text{trust}}, \omega_{\text{expert}})$, yielding $l_{\text{ED}} = t \cdot l_{\text{expert}}$.

\textbf{Theorem 2 (Method Comparison).} Let $n$ identifier categories be removed with confidence $p$ and the remaining $18-n$ categories be absent with confidence $q$, so that $l_{\text{SH}} = p^{n} q^{18-n}$. Expert Determination exceeds Safe Harbor exactly when $t > t^* = p^{n} q^{18-n}/l_{\text{expert}}$. With $p = 0.95$ and $l_{\text{expert}} = 0.90$: $n = 18$ gives $t^* = 0.441$; the Synthea scenario $n = 6$ with $q = 0.99$ gives $t^* = 0.724$. Fig.~\ref{fig:trust_crossover} shows both crossovers.

\emph{Proof.} Trust discount gives $l_{\text{ED}} = t \, l_{\text{expert}}$, so $l_{\text{ED}} > l_{\text{SH}}$ exactly when $t > p^{n} q^{18-n} / l_{\text{expert}}$; numerically $0.95^{18}/0.90 = 0.441$ and $0.95^{6} \cdot 0.99^{12} / 0.90 = 0.724$. $\square$

\input{figures/fig_trust_crossover}

\subsection{Operator H3: Minimum Necessary Rule}

HIPAA \S164.502(b) and \S164.514(d) require limiting PHI to the minimum necessary. We model three conditions:

\textbf{Definition 4.} $\text{MinNec}(\omega_{\text{role}}, \omega_{\text{purpose}}, \omega_{\text{scope}}) = J_\sqcap(\omega_{\text{role}}, \omega_{\text{purpose}}, \omega_{\text{scope}})$

\textbf{Theorem 3 (Weakest Link).} If any component has $l = 0$, then $l_{\text{MN}} = 0$.

\emph{Proof.} $l_{\text{MN}} = l_{\text{role}} \, l_{\text{purpose}} \, l_{\text{scope}}$ contains a zero factor. $\square$

\subsection{Operator H4: BAA Trust Chain}

HIPAA \S164.502(e) governs Business Associate Agreements. BAA chains arise naturally: Covered Entity $\to$ BA $\to$ Sub-BA.

\textbf{Definition 5.} $\text{BAAChain}(\omega_S, [(\tau_1, \pi_1), \ldots]) = \text{Prop}_n \circ \cdots \circ \text{Prop}_1(\omega_S)$, where each $\text{Prop}_i$ applies Compliance Propagation.

\textbf{Theorem 4 (Multiplicative Decay).} $l_{D_n} = l_S \cdot \prod_{i=1}^{n} (t_i \cdot p_i)$.

\emph{Proof.} Each link applies $\text{Prop}_i(\omega) = J_\sqcap(\tau_i, \pi_i, \omega)$, whose lawfulness is $t_i \, p_i$ times that of $\omega$, where $t_i$ and $p_i$ are the lawfulness components of $\tau_i$ and $\pi_i$; induction on $n$. $\square$

\subsection{Dual-Regime Composition}

\textbf{Definition 6.} $\text{DualRegime}(\omega_{\text{HIPAA}}, \omega_{\text{GDPR}}) = J_\sqcap(\omega_{\text{HIPAA}}, \omega_{\text{GDPR}})$

\textbf{Theorem 5.} (a)~$l_{\text{dual}} \leq \min(l_{\text{HIPAA}}, l_{\text{GDPR}})$. (b)~Commutative. (c)~Vacuous opinion in either regime forces $l_{\text{dual}} = 0$.

\emph{Proof.} (a)~$l_{\text{dual}} = l_{\text{HIPAA}} \, l_{\text{GDPR}}$ is at most each factor. (b)~Every component in Definition~2 is symmetric in its arguments. (c)~A vacuous opinion has $l = 0$, which zeroes the product. $\square$

\subsection{Carrying Opinions in FHIR R4}

FHIR JSON is not JSON-LD, so \texttt{jsonld-ex} carries an opinion inside FHIR R4 through the standard extension mechanism~\cite{hl7fhirr4}: a complex extension whose sub-extensions \texttt{belief}, \texttt{disbelief}, \texttt{uncertainty}, and \texttt{baseRate} hold \texttt{valueDecimal} values. A clinical-assertion opinion is attached to the element it qualifies through the JSON \texttt{\_element} convention, for instance \texttt{prediction.\_probabilityDecimal} of \texttt{RiskAssessment}, so the scalar probability stays intact for systems that ignore the extension. A compliance opinion about a whole resource or Bundle is carried in \texttt{meta.extension} under a distinct URL (\texttt{compliance-opinion}), with optional \texttt{regime} and \texttt{assessedAt} sub-extensions; \texttt{from\_fhir} and \texttt{to\_fhir} convert both carriers to and from the \texttt{jsonld-ex} document model. We exercised the compliance carrier on all 100 Synthea bundles: the PHI Classification opinion of each bundle, attached at the Bundle and Patient levels, survived JSON serialization and reload with zero component error; the extension occupies 393 bytes and grows a bundle by at most 1.1\%.

\section{Privacy-Preserving Compliance Assessment}
%===================================================================

Consider a hospital whose departments must compute an aggregate HIPAA compliance opinion, by Jurisdictional Meet, without any party learning another department's opinion.

Since $J_\sqcap$ requires only additions and multiplications (Eq.~\ref{eq:jmeet}), it is directly computable under the CKKS scheme~\cite{cheon2017ckks}. We encrypt each opinion component as a CKKS scalar and compute $\text{Enc}(l_\sqcap) = \text{Enc}(l_1) \otimes \text{Enc}(l_2)$ and $\text{Enc}(v_\sqcap) = \text{Enc}(v_1) \oplus \text{Enc}(v_2) \ominus \text{Enc}(v_1) \otimes \text{Enc}(v_2)$. After decryption, normalization enforces $l + v + u = 1$; the raw violation before normalization was $6.70 \times 10^{-13}$, near machine epsilon, so normalization is a safeguard rather than a correction.

\input{figures/tab_he_benchmarks}

The overhead is substantial: $15{,}186\times$ compared to plaintext computation. However, the absolute latency of 27\,ms per operation is acceptable for compliance assessment, which is not a real-time control task. We report this overhead prominently because honest characterization of limitations strengthens rather than weakens a feasibility claim.

\textbf{Deployment considerations.} The proof of concept uses TenSEAL over Microsoft SEAL with polynomial modulus degree 8192 (depths 1 and 2) or 16384 (depths 3 to 5), a 40-bit scale, and one 40-bit prime per multiplicative level. The threat model is an honest-but-curious aggregator: departments encrypt under the public key of the compliance authority, the aggregator evaluates $J_\sqcap$ on ciphertexts, and only the authority decrypts, so no department opinion is revealed to any other party. Each meet consumes one multiplicative level; without bootstrapping, a tree-structured aggregation of $D$ departments needs depth $\lceil \log_2 D \rceil$, so the depth-5 budget of Table~\ref{tab:he_benchmarks} covers 32 departments, and at the measured medians aggregating 32 departments takes about 0.5\,s single-threaded.

\section{Evaluation}
%===================================================================

\subsection{Operator Property Verification}

We verified Theorems~1 through 5 with the Hypothesis property-based testing framework: 10,000 random compliance opinions per operator across 33 properties, all passing with zero counterexamples, and $l + v + u = 1$ within $\varepsilon < 10^{-9}$ for every composition.

\subsection{Clinical Compliance Pipeline}

We evaluated the operators on 100 synthetic patient bundles generated by Synthea~\cite{walonoski2018synthea}, containing FHIR R4 resources across 32 types.

\textbf{PHI Classification.} All 100 patients contain exactly 6 HIPAA identifier categories (names, addresses, dates, phone numbers, SSNs, other identifiers). The resulting $l_{\text{PHI}} = 8.44 \times 10^{-9}$ confirms that raw patient data is correctly classified as PHI.

\textbf{Progressive De-identification.} Fig.~\ref{fig:progressive_deident} shows $l_{\text{PHI}}$ recovering as the six present identifiers are removed one by one, matching theory at every step.

\input{figures/fig_progressive_deident}

\textbf{De-identification Comparison.} Safe Harbor yields $l_{\text{SH}} = 0.652$ ($= 0.95^6 \cdot 0.99^{12}$). Expert Determination at trust $t = 0.85$ yields $l_{\text{ED}} = 0.765$. Expert exceeds Safe Harbor for all 100 patients, with trust threshold $t^* = 0.724$, validating Theorem~2.

\textbf{Cross-Resource Profiles.} Different FHIR resource types contain different identifier subsets (Table~\ref{tab:cross_resource}), producing $l_{\text{PHI}}$ values spanning eight orders of magnitude, from $1.10 \times 10^{-3}$ (Condition, 2~identifiers) to $2.34 \times 10^{-11}$ (Claim, 8~identifiers).

\textbf{Sensitivity to assumed confidences.} A pre-registered study varied the per-identifier confidences fixed above. On a grid of removal confidence $p \in [0.80, 0.999]$ and absence confidence $q \in [0.90, 0.999]$, the Expert Determination advantage at $t = 0.85$ holds for every $p \leq 0.95$ and reverses only when $p$ and $q$ both reach 0.99 ($l_{\text{SH}} = 0.835 > 0.765$). With confidences drawn per identifier from Beta distributions around the nominal values (10,000 draws), $l_{\text{SH}}$ spans 0.580 to 0.721 (5th to 95th percentile) at concentration 100 and 0.494 to 0.798 at concentration 20, and the expert advantage holds in 99.8\% and 88.9\% of draws. With identifier presence sampled at the empirical rates of the seven resource-type profiles, the 95th percentile of $l_{\text{PHI}}$ is $5.8 \times 10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Temporal Decay of Compliance Assessments}

HIPAA requires ongoing maintenance of safeguards: covered entities must
review and modify their security measures as needed
(45~CFR~164.306(e))~\cite{hipaa_security}. Binary tools provide no mechanism
to trigger reassessment: a two-year-old PASS is indistinguishable from
today's PASS. The compliance algebra models assessment staleness through
opinion decay~\cite{josang2016subjective}, where evidence migrates from
belief and disbelief into uncertainty over time.

Table~\ref{tab:temporal_decay} shows a strong initial assessment
($l = 0.900$, $v = 0.050$, $u = 0.050$) decaying with an exponential half-life
of one year. After two years, lawfulness confidence drops to 0.225 while
uncertainty grows to 0.762: the assessment has not become a known
violation ($v = 0.013$), the evidence has become stale. The projected
probability $P(\omega)$ declines from 0.925 to 0.606, crossing below a
hypothetical reassessment threshold of 0.70 on day 397, between one and two years.

\subsection{End-to-End Clinical Workflow}

We trace a compliance lifecycle for one synthetic patient to show how the
operators compose.

\textbf{Step 1: PHI Classification.} A Synthea patient record contains
six identifier categories (names, addresses, dates, phone numbers, SSN,
and a facility identifier). PHI Classification yields
$\omega_{\text{PHI}} = (8.44 \times 10^{-9},\; 0.9999,\; 7.76 \times 10^{-7},\; 3.81 \times 10^{-6})$
with $P = 8.45 \times 10^{-9}$: the record is PHI and requires protection
under the Privacy Rule before any sharing.

\textbf{Step 2: De-identification.} Safe Harbor removal of the six
identifiers at $p = 0.95$ yields $l_{\text{SH}} = 0.652$; an available
expert ($l_{\text{expert}} = 0.90$, $t = 0.85$) yields
$l_{\text{ED}} = 0.765$, 17\% higher, quantifying the value of Expert
Determination over mechanical removal.

\textbf{Step 3: BAA Chain.} Sharing the de-identified data with a
laboratory (BA) through one BAA link ($t = 0.90$, $p = 0.95$) gives
$l_{D_1} = 0.557$, a 14.5\% degradation from $l = 0.652$; a
subcontracted cloud analytics provider (sub-BA) with weaker safeguards
($t = 0.75$, $p = 0.85$) degrades the chain to $l_{D_2} = 0.355$.

\textbf{Step 4: Dual-Regime.} The laboratory's EU affiliate requests
access for a joint trial. With
$\omega_{\text{GDPR}} = (0.450, 0.250, 0.300, 0.30)$, reflecting lawful
basis and data minimization requirements, the composition gives
$l_{\text{dual}} = 0.293$, a 55\% reduction from the HIPAA assessment
alone; the composite $(0.293, 0.475, 0.232)$ shows violation risk
($v = 0.475$) exceeding lawfulness, so additional safeguards or a more
favorable GDPR assessment are needed before cross-border transfer.

Binary tools can only report PASS or FAIL at each stage; the algebra
preserves the full epistemic state through every transformation.

\subsection{Comparison with Binary and Other Uncertainty-Aware Approaches}

Table~\ref{tab:binary_scenarios} presents five scenarios where binary assessment collapses distinct epistemic states. The strongest distinction is Scenario~2 (new vendor): binary tools assign the same FAIL to complete ignorance ($u = 1.0$) and known violation ($v = 1.0$), though the projected probabilities differ ($P = 0.5$ vs.\ $P = 0.0$) and the appropriate responses (gather evidence vs.\ block access) are entirely different.

A binomial opinion is in bijection with a Beta distribution, so a Bayesian representation is a reparameterization and the difference lies in the operator set (meet, trust discount, decay). $J_\sqcap$ coincides with the conjunction of independent belief functions, so Dempster-Shafer theory is the closest relative and the difference is the combination rule: for the two auditors of Scenario~1, Dempster's rule discards a conflict mass of 0.685 and returns $(0.571, 0.397, 0.032)$, and SL cumulative fusion returns $(0.500, 0.447, 0.053)$, whereas $J_\sqcap$ keeps the conflict visible as $v = 0.810$; interval probabilities ($[0.85, 0.95]$ and $[0.10, 0.20]$) expose the conflict as an empty intersection but yield no combined value. Scalar probabilities, fuzzy memberships, and ordinal risk levels cannot separate ignorance from conflict, and none supplies trust discounting or temporal decay. The algebra's own costs are the independence assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:independence}, base rates that must be supplied, and a binary frame per proposition.

\subsection{Independence Assumptions}
\label{sec:independence}

PHI Classification assumes independent identifier presence; positive correlation (identifiers co-occur) means true risk exceeds our estimate, a non-conservative bias. Minimum Necessary assumes independent role, purpose, and scope; since roles imply purposes, independence overstates uncertainty, a conservative bias. Bias directions are documented so results can be interpreted accordingly.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%===================================================================

Limitations include: (1)~the HIPAA-to-algebra mapping involves interpretive choices that compliance experts may dispute; (2)~all base rates are illustrative, not empirically calibrated against enforcement data, though they change only $P$ and no $(l, v, u)$; (3)~the HE proof-of-concept demonstrates feasibility but not deployment readiness, with $15{,}186\times$ overhead; (4)~Synthea generates uniform patient demographics, limiting variance in the empirical evaluation.

\textbf{Implications for compliance practice.} A compliance officer reads an opinion as three signals and distinguishes ``probably compliant'' ($l$ high, $u$ low) from ``uncertain'' ($u$ high) from ``conflicting evidence'' ($l$ and $v$ both elevated), each with its own response: proceed, gather evidence before deciding, or investigate, since more evidence does not resolve a conflict. Table~\ref{tab:temporal_decay} turns staleness into a reassessment trigger, and Theorem~4 locates the link that dominates degradation in a BAA chain (the billing link in Table~\ref{tab:binary_scenarios}), a basis for supply chain risk management consistent with \S164.502(e)~\cite{hipaa_privacy}. Existing checklist scores map directly to $l$, $v$, and $u$ (pass, fail, and not-assessed fractions), which the operators then compose across de-identification steps, business associates, and regimes.

\section{Conclusion}
%===================================================================

We presented a HIPAA compliance algebra that models healthcare privacy compliance as uncertain epistemic states, with four operators grounded in specific HIPAA provisions, proofs of their properties, and property-based verification. We demonstrated dual-regime GDPR and HIPAA composition, a FHIR R4 carrier for opinions, and a proof-of-concept for privacy-preserving compliance aggregation using homomorphic encryption that stays within $1.19 \times 10^{-7}$ of plaintext for a single operation and within $4.0 \times 10^{-6}$ across chain depths up to five.

Future work includes empirical calibration of base rates against enforcement data, extension to additional healthcare regulations, and collaboration with legal scholars to validate the HIPAA-to-algebra mapping. The implementation is available as part of \texttt{jsonld-ex} on PyPI.
